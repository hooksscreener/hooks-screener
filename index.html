◈ About This Screener
Quality · Momentum · Edge
This screener was built around one idea: finding great businesses at the right price, at the right time. It doesn't just ask whether a stock looks good today. It asks whether the business is fundamentally strong, whether the market is starting to recognize that strength, whether the stock is currently offering a real discount, and whether the selling pressure has clearly stopped. All four questions have to be answered at the same time before the screener signals a buy.
The Core Idea
Fundamentals tell you why a stock deserves a position. Momentum tells you when to actually buy it. Most investors focus on one or the other. This system requires both.
"Is this a great business, in a real uptrend, currently on sale — and has the selling pressure just stopped?" That is the question this screener is designed to answer.
The Four Phases
Each stock must clear all four phases simultaneously to generate a signal.
Phase 1
Value — Is This a Good Business?
Inspired by Warren Buffett's approach to value investing. This phase only looks at the quality of the company itself — how efficiently it generates profit from the money invested in it, how much real cash it produces, and how much debt it carries. A stock can't pass the later phases without clearing this one first.
Phase 2
Trend — Is the Market Agreeing?
A great business can stay flat for years if the market hasn't recognized it yet. This phase looks at price behavior — specifically whether the stock has shown sustained strength over the past year and whether its long-term average price is continuing to rise. Both have to be true.
Phase 3
Dip — Is It Currently on Sale?
Once a business is confirmed as high quality and in a real uptrend, this phase asks whether it has pulled back recently. The goal is to find a better entry point than chasing the stock near its highs. A pullback that is too shallow may not offer much of a discount. A pullback that is too deep may mean something is actually wrong. The focus is on the healthy middle ground.
Phase 4
Pivot — Has the Selling Stopped?
A stock can pass the first three phases and just sit there indefinitely. Phase 4 is the actual buy signal. It looks for the moment when selling pressure has clearly exhausted itself and buyers are beginning to step back in. Two independent momentum indicators are used so that a real pivot is confirmed rather than just noise in one signal.
Key Concepts Explained Simply
Return on Capital
Measures how efficiently a company turns the money invested in it into profit. Think of it as the business's batting average — how much does it generate for every dollar it's given to work with? A company that can consistently generate strong returns on its capital is one that compounds value over time.
Free Cash Flow
The cash left over after a business pays for everything it needs to keep running and growing. Free cash flow is harder to manipulate than reported earnings because cash is real. A company that generates strong free cash flow relative to its market value is genuinely producing cash, not just reporting accounting profits.
Debt Level
Debt amplifies both gains and losses. A company with manageable debt can survive a difficult year without being forced into damaging decisions. Too much debt turns an ordinary business downturn into a serious problem. This screener looks for companies that use debt responsibly rather than depending on it.
Momentum
Price momentum reflects the market's collective recognition of a business's strength. Markets don't price information instantly. A stock with sustained price strength over the past year is one where recognition is building. Combining that with strong fundamentals gives you a stock where business quality and market recognition are starting to align.
The Dip and the Pivot
Even in a strong uptrend, stocks pull back. The dip phase identifies when a stock has pulled back into a range that historically represents a healthy pause rather than a trend reversal. The pivot phase confirms the pullback is over — that selling has exhausted itself and buyers are returning. Buying into a dip without a confirmed pivot is catching a falling knife. Waiting for the pivot is what makes the entry disciplined rather than speculative.
Portfolio Philosophy
Concentration
15–20 positions. Concentrated enough that each pick matters. Diversified enough to survive any single mistake.
Review cadence
Quarterly, timed around earnings season — when fundamentals actually update.
Holding period
8–24 months. Long enough for a thesis to play out. Short enough to stay disciplined.
When to sell
Three explicit triggers defined in advance — fundamental quality deteriorates, debt rises to a concerning level, or the long-term price trend breaks. Defined in advance so emotion doesn't make the decision.
What This Is Not
This screener is not a prediction system. It does not guarantee a stock will go up. It is a disciplined framework for identifying businesses worth owning, at moments when the market is offering a reasonable entry point.
